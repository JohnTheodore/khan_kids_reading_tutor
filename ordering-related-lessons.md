# Khan Academy Kids lessons related to ordering in reading

This analysis searched all **653 unique ELA title strings** and all **1,339 grade placements** captured from Class Reports → All Progress. It distinguishes sound/letter ordering for decoding from unrelated narrative or sentence sequencing.

> **Status:** Inventory and sequencing analysis based on the captured archive.
> It is not the live assignment list. See [`reading-path.md`](reading-path.md)
> for the current mastery-gated route and queue.

## Main finding

Khan Kids has no lesson explicitly titled **phoneme segmentation**, **sound order**, or **left-to-right tracking**. Its closest and most useful lessons are organized under blending, phoneme position, phoneme manipulation, CVC words, and consonant blends.

For a child who already knows many grapheme–phoneme pairs, the most direct route is:

1. Blend larger spoken chunks.
2. Blend onset and rime.
3. Blend three phonemes without letters.
4. Identify first, middle, and last phonemes.
5. Connect those positions to letters in CVC words.
6. Manipulate phonemes and preserve their order.
7. Extend the sequence to consonant blends and longer words.

## Recommended progression

Every title in stages 1–3 has **Main, Practice 1, Practice 2, and Basic** variants unless noted. The report titles do not establish a reliable difficulty order among those variants; previews or a short trial are needed before ordering the variants themselves.

### 1. Syllable-sized chunks

- **Hear Syllables** — notice that a spoken word contains an ordered series of chunks.
- **Blend Syllables** — combine spoken chunks in the presented order.
- **Delete Syllables** — manipulate a sequence after blending; use after the first two.

Locations: Preschool (Age 3), Preschool (Age 4), and Kindergarten. `Delete Syllables` begins at Age 4.

### 2. Onset and rime

- **Blend Sounds 1** — combine the initial sound/chunk with the rest of a word.
- **Beginning Sounds 1** — identify the first part of that sequence.
- **Word Families** — hold the final rime constant while changing the beginning.

Locations: Preschool (Age 3), Preschool (Age 4), and Kindergarten.

### 3. Three-phoneme words

- **Blend Sounds 2** — the clearest early lesson for combining a three-sound sequence into a word.
- **Beginning Sounds 2** — locate the first phoneme in a three-phoneme word.
- **Words: End Sound** — locate the final phoneme in a three-phoneme word.
- **Make New Words** — add a phoneme while retaining the sequence; use after blending and position work.

Locations: Preschool (Age 3), Preschool (Age 4), and Kindergarten. `Make New Words` begins at Age 4.

### 4. Grapheme position in CVC words

These connect ordered sound positions to written letters. Each title has **Main, Practice 1, and Practice 2**.

Beginning-position groups—use mainly as a quick check because Student A already has substantial beginning-sound practice:

- **Words with b, c, d**
- **Words with f, g, h**
- **Words with j, k, l**
- **Words with m, n, p**
- **Other Words**

Ending-position groups:

- **Words with b & d**
- **Words with g & k**
- **Words with m & n**
- **Words with p & s**
- **Words with t & x**

Middle-position groups:

- **Words with a**
- **Words with e**
- **Words with i**
- **Words with o**
- **Words with u**

Locations: Preschool (Age 3), Preschool (Age 4), and Kindergarten.

### 5. Explicit first/middle/last analysis

These 1st Grade titles most directly test whether the child understands that changing sound position changes the word:

- **Beginning Sound**
- **Middle Sound**
- **Ending Sound**
- **First & Last Sound**
- **Isolate All Sounds** — strongest match for analyzing the complete ordered phoneme sequence; Main, Practice 1, and Practice 2 only.
- **1-Syllable Words**
- **2-Syllable Words**

All except `Isolate All Sounds` have Main, Practice 1, Practice 2, and Basic.

### 6. Preserve consonant order in blends

Use only after three-phoneme blending and isolation are secure. These require retaining two adjacent consonants in the correct order.

1st Grade titles:

- **Blends: st**
- **Blends: sp**
- **st, sp, sk, sm**
- **sl, sn, sc, sw**
- **Blends: bl**
- **gl, pl, bl, cl**
- **Blends: tr**
- **Blends: fr**
- **gr, tr, dr, pr**
- **bl, br, gl, gr**
- **cl, cr, fl, fr**

The 2nd Grade report has broader review groups: `st, sp, sk, sm`; `sc, sl, sn, sw`; `bl, cl, fl`; `gl, pl, sl`; `dr, gr, pr, tr`; `br, cr, fr`; and `qu, tw`.

## Student A's evidence

- She has **64 scored Beginning Letter Sounds activity records**, mostly strong. Notable weaker results include `Beginning Sound n — Practice 2` at 60%, `Beginning Sound w — Main` at 67%, and `Beginning Sound k — Main` at 78%.
- She has only three scored Ending Letter Sounds records: `Ending Sound b — Main` 100%, `Ending Sound b — Basic` 100%, and `Ending Sound d — Basic` 100%.
- `Blend Sounds 2 — Basic` is 85%.
- `Words with b, c, d — Main` is 92%.
- The other core sequencing lessons above have blank report cells. This means **unattempted/no displayed evidence**, not failure.

This pattern suggests that more isolated beginning-sound drills would add less information than ordered blending, middle/final position, and whole-sequence isolation work.

## Lessons about other kinds of ordering

These genuinely teach ordering, but not the sound order needed for decoding:

- **First, Then, Next** — Kindergarten informative-writing sequence.
- **Retell - Sequencing** — Kindergarten and 1st Grade narrative event order.
- **Arrange Sentences** — 2nd Grade word order/syntax.
- **Sentence Parts** — 1st Grade sentence structure.

They should not substitute for `Blend Sounds 1`, `Blend Sounds 2`, positional-sound lessons, or `Isolate All Sounds` when the goal is learning to decode.
