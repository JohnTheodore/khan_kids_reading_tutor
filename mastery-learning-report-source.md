# Research record: mastery learning for Student A's Khan Kids reading plan

- **Audience:** Parent managing Student A's Khan Kids assignments
- **Research date:** 2026-09-08
- **Scope:** A mastery-learning workflow using only lessons and reports available
  inside Khan Academy Kids
- **Constraint:** No off-screen instruction or activities will be proposed

## Executive answer

Use a feedback loop, not a one-shot score ladder: assign one variant, inspect its
attempt history, provide an in-app corrective when the evidence is below the
chosen mastery standard, reassess, and advance only after mastery. Student A's
Basic-first rung order is **Basic → Main → Practice 1 → Practice 2**. Remove each
mastered assignment from her queue but preserve its attempt and decision record.

For this family, 100% on a completed attempt is sufficient evidence to master a
variant. A single 90–99% result is provisional and requires another result of at
least 90%. Scores below 90% do not promote. This cutoff is an explicit local
decision: neither Bloom nor Khan Kids validates it as a universal psychometric
definition of mastery.

## Evidence synthesis

### Essential features, not merely a high cutoff

Bloom frames aptitude as the time a learner needs to reach mastery rather than a
fixed ceiling on what the learner can achieve. His approach requires:

- predetermined, criterion-referenced objectives;
- small, logically sequenced learning units;
- brief formative checks after units;
- feedback that identifies what has and has not been learned;
- corrective learning matched to the identified gap;
- another opportunity to demonstrate mastery before moving on; and
- variable time and support while the achievement standard remains stable.

This means “repeat until the number is high” is incomplete. The score must alter
the next instructional decision, particularly when it reveals a prerequisite
gap. Bloom also cautions against applying one procedure mechanically across all
learners and subjects.

### Evidence of effectiveness and limits

Kulik, Kulik, and Bangert-Drowns synthesized 108 controlled evaluations. Their
abstract reports positive effects on examination performance across colleges,
high schools, and upper elementary grades, with stronger effects for weaker
students. Effects varied by mastery procedure, research design, and content;
mastery approaches could also require more time. This is supportive evidence for
the general cycle, but it is not direct validation of a particular Khan Kids
score cutoff or of a one-child early-literacy protocol.

### What Khan Kids scores establish

Khan Kids' official progress-report guidance defines:

- green, 80–100%: strong understanding;
- yellow, 50–79%: developing and potentially needing more practice; and
- red, below 50%: needing additional practice, with Basic as a possible support.

The report permits review of all attempts for an activity. Those bands are useful
screening signals, but Khan Kids does not call every green result “mastered” or
publish reliability evidence showing that one green attempt warrants promotion.

Khan's generic activity guidance says Main first, Basic for students needing
support, Practice 1 after Main mastery, and Practice 2 after Practice 1 mastery.
The Basic-first family ladder in this repository is a parent-selected adaptation.

## Operational decisions

1. One active rung per skill family.
2. 100% once masters that lesson variant.
3. 90–99% once is provisional; a second score ≥90% masters the variant.
4. Below 90% does not promote.
5. Scores below 80% trigger a prerequisite or closely targeted corrective inside
   Khan Kids before reassessment.
6. A mastered assignment is removed after its score and decision are recorded.
7. The next rung is treated as a transfer/generalization check. A result below
   80% pauses advancement and triggers correction at that rung.

## Limitations and uncertainty

- App scores are brief performance samples and may be affected by guessing,
  attention, item familiarity, or assistance.
- A single 100% is less reliable than independent demonstrations over time. We
  nevertheless accept it for variant-level mastery as explicitly requested, and
  use the next rung as a practical transfer check.
- Repeating an identical item set may inflate confidence. Variant progression is
  preferable when available because it changes support or item presentation.
- The 90% and two-attempt rules are conservative operational judgments, not
  thresholds established by the cited research.
- The meta-analysis is not specific to preschool phonological-awareness apps;
  conclusions should remain modest and be revised as Student A's longitudinal
  results accumulate.
