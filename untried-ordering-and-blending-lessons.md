**Audience:** Student A's parent

## Direct answer

The complete ELA archive contains 1,339 reported lesson placements, 2,956
assignable activity placements after expanding variants, and 653 globally
unique titles. After deduplicating repeated grade placements and reconciling
both Student A evidence sources, the ordering/blending screen contains:

- **39 completely untried lesson families**;
- **9 partly tried families with untried higher variants**; and
- **153 untried title/variant activities** in total.

These are inventory results, not a recommendation to assign all of them now.
Ordering is used narrowly here to mean retaining or identifying sound position
within a spoken or printed word. Narrative sequence, sentence order, grammar,
passive stories, sight-word memorization, and isolated letter drills are
excluded.

This boundary follows the evidence base: blending means combining a sequence of
sounds into a word, while segmentation and manipulation preserve awareness of
the component sounds and their positions. IES recommends progressing from sound
segments and letter links into decoding, including left-to-right blending. The
National Reading Panel identifies blending, segmenting, deletion, and onset-rime
manipulation as relevant phonemic-awareness tasks. [IES Foundational Skills
Practice Guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/21/Published),
[National Reading Panel findings](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/publications/pubs/nrp/findings).

## Method and limitations

“Untried” means no scored result for the title/variant in either
[`data/reading-ela-archive.json`](data/reading-ela-archive.json) or
[`student-records/student-a-lesson-attempts.csv`](student-records/student-a-lesson-attempts.csv).
Repeated placements across Preschool, Kindergarten, and later grades were
deduplicated by title and variant.

Classification is high-confidence for explicit `Blend`, phoneme-position,
phoneme-manipulation, CVC, and consonant-blend taxonomy. It is only moderate for
`Hear Syllables`, `1-Syllable Words`, and `2-Syllable Words` because Khan's
archive exposes titles and skill groups but not prose descriptions or every
prompt. Short-vowel identification, digraphs, vowel teams, spelling rules,
decodable books, and reading-fluency lessons may support later decoding, but
they were excluded because their archive labels do not establish that they
teach the ordering/blending operation itself.
